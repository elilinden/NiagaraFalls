A note on timing

This is Memorial Day weekend, the unofficial start of summer tourism at the Falls. Buses, hotels, and Maid of the Mist boats fill fast and prices climb the longer you wait. Lock in everything within the next week. The 6:30 AM OurBus puts you at the Falls by mid-afternoon Friday, with enough light left to see your first rainbow before dinner. For the return, the 11:00 AM bus on Sunday gets you home around 8 PM. Both rides have free Wi-Fi and outlets, which means you get eight uninterrupted hours of side-by-side window time.

day one
Friday · May 22, 2026

Arrival & First Light

A long ride that ends with the loudest welcome of your life. By dinner you'll be standing at the brink.

5:30 AM
Pre-dawn
depart UWS $25 cab

Cab to the GWB

Wake up too early. Coffee in a thermos. Take a yellow cab or Uber from the UWS up to the George Washington Bridge Bus Station at 175th and Fort Washington. About fifteen minutes at this hour. The A train works too if you want to save the fare; the station is right at the 175th Street stop.

Pack the night before. Backpacks only, no checked bags needed. Throw in two ponchos (you'll get one free on the boat but have backups).
6:30 AM
Departure
★ on the road ~$50 each

Board OurBus to Niagara ♡

Eight and a half hours up the Hudson, across the Mohawk Valley, past Syracuse, past Rochester, past every horizon line you've ever seen. Free Wi-Fi, outlets at every seat, complimentary water. Sit on the right side for better river views on the way up. Bring a book she's been meaning to read aloud.

Snack run: No real meal stops. Pack sandwiches from Zabar's or Citarella, a thermos, and something sweet. The bus does pull over once in Syracuse if you need to stretch.
3:00 PM
Arrival
drop-off free

Step off the bus, hear the falls

OurBus drops at Old Main Street, charter zone behind the Visitor Center. You are now literally one block from the entrance to Niagara Falls State Park. Walk to your hotel, drop bags, and breathe. You can already hear the water from here.

3:45 PM
Settle in
check in

Hotel check-in

Whichever spot you've booked (see the Stay section below), drop the bags, change into something you don't mind getting damp. Comfortable shoes, layers, a light jacket for the gorge breeze. Take a quick photo at the door before you walk out, you'll want it later.

4:30 PM
First view
★ the brink free

Prospect Point Observation Tower ♡

Walk to the edge. Prospect Point is the closest you can stand to the American Falls without getting wet. Take the elevator down to the gorge for a bird's-eye view of the whole horseshoe across the river. Save the boat ride for after, the anticipation matters.

Worth knowing: The Niagara Falls State Park is the oldest state park in America. Free to enter, open 24/7. The Observation Tower is $1.25 per person. That is not a typo.
5:00 PM
Boat
★ the moment $30.25 each

Maid of the Mist ♡

The most famous boat tour in North America, running since 1846. They hand you a blue poncho. The boat noses out into the gorge. You drift past the American Falls. Then the captain swings you straight into the curve of Horseshoe Falls and the world disappears into white. Twenty minutes. You will be soaked. You will not stop smiling.

Late-day trick: Lines are shortest in the late afternoon (after 4 PM crowds clear). Tickets are first-come-first-served even online, so just walk up. Last boats leave around 6 PM in May.
7:00 PM
Dinner
first dinner $$

The Red Coach Inn

A hundred-year-old Tudor-style inn 500 yards from the Falls. Walk-in friendly, but reserve. Order the prime rib if you want classic, the seafood if you want lighter. Ask for a patio table if the weather holds, you can hear the water from there.

Want something different? Savor at the Niagara Falls Culinary Institute is the modern alternative: student chefs, open kitchen, locally sourced. Same price range, very different vibe.
9:00 PM
Night falls
★ illumination free

The Falls in color

Every night at 8 PM, twenty-one xenon spotlights come on across the gorge and the water turns rose, then violet, then turquoise, then gold. Walk the path along the rim. There are benches. The mist makes everything halo.

10:00 PM
Fireworks
★ end of night one free

Fireworks over the gorge ♡

Five minutes. They go off from inside the gorge, between the American and Horseshoe Falls, and they reflect off the water and the mist and the lit-up cataract behind them. It's the longest-running fireworks series in Canada. May 22 is the eighth night of the season, so the crew is sharp. Best US-side view: Terrapin Point on Goat Island.

Heads up: Fireworks run nightly from May 15 through October 12, weather permitting. Strong winds cancel; check 1-877-642-7275.
11:00 PM
Goodnight

Walk back, slowly

Hold hands. Don't talk if you don't need to. The water is still crashing in the dark behind you.

day two
Saturday · May 23, 2026

Into the Thunder

The big day. You'll get wet twice, walk through a cave of wind, and see the Falls from three countries' worth of angles.

7:30 AM
Wake
slow start

Breakfast in bed (or close to it)

Most hotels on the US side have an included breakfast. Eat well. Carbs. Coffee. You'll need it. If you'd rather walk: Tim Hortons on Niagara Street is 1990s strip-mall but it's a Niagara classic, and the bagels here are weirdly good.

9:00 AM
Cave of the Winds
★ get wet again $23 each

Cave of the Winds, Hurricane Deck ♡

Take an elevator 175 feet down into the gorge, then walk up wooden walkways toward the base of Bridal Veil Falls. The closest deck (the Hurricane Deck) puts you ten feet from a falling sheet of water moving at 68 mph. They give you a poncho and special sandals. You will get drenched and you will not care.

Book first thing: Cave of the Winds uses timed tickets. Reserve the 9:30 AM slot online before you go to skip the morning rush.
11:00 AM
Walk it off
stroll free

Goat Island & Three Sisters

Cross the bridge to Goat Island, the green sliver between the American and Horseshoe Falls. The Three Sisters Islands are tiny stepping-stone islets in the upper rapids; you can walk all three. Terrapin Point at the far end has the single best view of the Canadian Horseshoe Falls from the US side.

12:30 PM
Lunch
refuel $$

Top of the Falls Restaurant

Right inside the State Park on Goat Island, with a wall of windows looking onto Horseshoe Falls. Seasonal, casual, mid-range. The view does most of the work; the food is solid American. Reserve a window table.

Lighter option: Pack lunch from the morning and picnic at the Three Sisters. There are benches and zero crowds.
2:00 PM
Crossing
★ if you have passports $1 toll

Walk across the Rainbow Bridge to Canada ♡

The American side gives you intimacy with the Falls; the Canadian side gives you the postcard. Walk over the Rainbow Bridge (toll: 50¢ on foot, $4 by car). Bring passports. The crossing is twenty minutes including the booth, and the first view you get when you reach Queen Victoria Park is the one that makes people cry.

No passports? Skip Canada and do the Niagara Power Vista (free, fascinating Tesla/Edison history) and the Whirlpool State Park hike instead. The plan still works.
3:00 PM
Behind
canada side ~$33 USD each

Journey Behind the Falls

From Table Rock Centre, an elevator drops 125 feet through solid rock to a tunnel that takes you behind the Horseshoe Falls. Two viewing portals let you stand directly behind the curtain of falling water. Twenty thousand gallons a second, three feet from your face. The other side has nothing like this.

5:00 PM
Skyline
altitude ~$22 USD each

Skylon Tower observation deck

775 feet up. The observation deck wraps the entire tower; you'll see both Falls, the gorge, the river, and on a clear day all the way to Toronto. The sunset window in late May lands around 8:30 PM. Time it.

7:00 PM
The big dinner
★ celebrate him $$$

Skylon Revolving Dining Room ♡

Or save money and eat at Queen Victoria Place Restaurant on the patio overlooking the Falls. This is the graduation dinner. Toast him. Tell him every reason you're proud of him. He just finished law school. He's earned every word.

Reservation note: Book the Revolving Dining Room two weeks out. Ask for a window table on the inner ring, the one that revolves. The whole room turns once an hour, so you'll see the Falls illuminated from above.
10:00 PM
Fireworks, take two
★ best seat in the house free

Queen Victoria Park, front row ♡

If you're already in Canada this is the move. Queen Victoria Park has the closest, best, free view of the fireworks, looking right up at them as they burst over the gorge. Spread out a jacket on the lawn. Lean against him. The Falls are illuminated behind you, the fireworks are above the water, the show lasts five minutes and the memory lasts decades.

11:00 PM
Back across

Walk back over the bridge

The Rainbow Bridge is open 24/7. The crossing back to the US is faster than the way out. Goodnight to a long, soaked, perfect day.

day three
Sunday · May 24, 2026

One Last Rainbow

A slow morning, one final adventure, and the long quiet ride home with all of it still in your hair.

6:30 AM
Sunrise
★ if you can free

Sunrise rainbows at Prospect Point ♡

Set one alarm. The first sunlight of the day catches the mist at the base of the American Falls and throws a rainbow that sometimes runs the full width of the gorge. It is empty out there at 6:30 AM. Just you, her, the water, and a half-mile of color.

Trade-off: If you want to sleep in instead, that's also fine; just push everything below back an hour. You earned it.
8:30 AM
Breakfast
slow morning $

Breakfast somewhere with windows

The Hard Rock Cafe across from the State Park does a decent breakfast and you'll be in and out fast. For something prettier, the Sheraton at the Falls has a tableside view. Pack: water, sunscreen, layers; the gorge wind picks up by mid-morning.

10:00 AM
One more
★ choose your final adventure varies

Pick one big thing

Three options, depending on the kind of last hurrah you want.

1. Helicopter tour with Rainbow Air ($120 each, 10 minutes, ridiculous views). The kind of thing you do once.

2. Whirlpool State Park hike, four miles north (free). Trail down into the gorge to where the river turns 90 degrees and forms a permanent whirlpool. Quiet, cinematic, no crowds.

3. Niagara Wine Trail (US side, ~$60 each in tastings). Twenty wineries within thirty minutes; the Marjim Manor and Schulze are excellent. Or cross to Canada for Niagara-on-the-Lake (Peller Estates, Trius), which is the more famous wine region.

Romantic dark horse: The Whirlpool Aero Car (Canadian side, $19 each), a 1916 cable car that crosses the gorge at the whirlpool. Ten minutes, terrifying, beautiful.
12:30 PM
Last lunch
final meal $$

Wine on Third

A small, intimate spot two blocks from the Falls with high-end tapas and a serious wine list. Order a flight to share. Or for something casual: Anchor Bar (the original home of the Buffalo wing, opened 1935) is a 25-minute Uber to Buffalo and worth it for the history. Great if you've decided to skip the wine trail.

2:30 PM
Last walk
say goodbye free

One more walk along the rim

From your hotel, walk slowly back along the State Park promenade. Take the photo you've been meaning to take. Pick up a small, unironic souvenir. There's a vintage carriage in the park; ride it once. Look at the water for a long time. You're going to miss it.

4:00 PM
Bus stop
load up

Back to Old Main Street

The same charter zone behind the Visitor Center. Be there fifteen minutes early. OurBus has a 4:00 PM departure most Sundays; confirm the day-of times when you book. Charge phones. Stretch.

4:15 PM
Homeward
★ the long quiet

Bus south, sun behind you ♡

Eight hours back. Sit on the left this time, the sunset will be on that side as you drop south past the Finger Lakes. Sleep on his shoulder. Watch a movie together with one earbud each. Eat the leftover snacks. Don't say much. You'll be back at GW Bridge by midnight, in your own bed by 1 AM Monday, the sound of the water still in your ears for at least a week.

12:30 AM
Home

Back on the UWS

Drop bags. Don't unpack. Tell each other one favorite moment before you fall asleep. Congratulations, counselor.

Field Notes

Tips, Tricks & Quiet Knowing

From travel forums, locals, and the kind of hard-won knowledge that saves you twenty minutes in line and forty bucks on a poncho you didn't need.

01

The Discover Niagara Shuttle is free.

A complimentary trolley runs along the Niagara River corridor, hitting Old Fort Niagara, the Power Vista, the Aquarium, and back to the State Park. Free, every 30 minutes, late May through October. Saves Ubers.

02

Bring real ponchos.

The disposable ones from the boat tour will fly off you in the gorge wind. Pack reusable ponchos from Amazon ($10 each) and bring an extra dry shirt. The Hurricane Deck at Cave of the Winds will soak the inside of your jeans.

03

Niagara Falls State Park is free.

No entrance fee, ever, 24/7. You only pay for individual attractions inside (Maid of the Mist, Cave of the Winds, Observation Tower). You can walk the rim and see all three Falls without spending a dollar.

04

Get the Niagara USA Discovery Pass.

$53 per adult, includes Maid of the Mist, Cave of the Winds, Observation Tower, Aquarium of Niagara, and Niagara Gorge trolley. Saves about $25 if you do all of those, which you will.

05

Passports = many more options.

The Canadian side has the iconic head-on view, Journey Behind the Falls, the Skylon Tower, Clifton Hill, and the best fireworks viewing. Pack passports. The Rainbow Bridge crossing is fast except on Saturday afternoon, when you may want to walk back rather than wait in car traffic.

06

Memorial Day weekend = book everything now.

Hotel prices climb 30-50% over a regular weekend, and the cheap rooms vanish first. Boats and Cave of the Winds tickets sell out for prime time slots. Lock everything in this week.

07

Morning is your friend.

Most tourists do Maid of the Mist between 10 AM and 2 PM. Lines shrink dramatically before 9:30 AM and after 4:30 PM. Day 1 in this plan uses the late slot; Day 2's Cave of the Winds uses the early slot.

08

Phones go dumb at the border.

Crossing into Canada, your phone may pick up a Canadian cell tower and roam. Toggle airplane mode on as you cross, or set up T-Mobile/Verizon's free Canada day pass before you leave NYC. You don't need data with the Falls in front of you anyway.

09

Niagara is dim after 11 PM.

Once the fireworks end, the tourist district shuts fast. The 24-hour exceptions: the State Park (always open), the Casino, and the Tim Hortons on Niagara St. Plan dinner to end before 10 PM so you're at the fireworks vantage point in time.

10

The bus goes both ways for cheaper than you think.

Round-trip on OurBus runs around $80-100 per person if you book together. Greyhound is $110-120. Booking 7+ days ahead is the difference between $39 and $89 a leg.

11

Charge in advance.

Bring portable batteries. The bus has outlets, but at the park you'll be photographing constantly and there's nowhere to plug in. Two 10000 mAh banks split between you covers the whole weekend.

12

The Cave of the Winds sandals are gone.

As of 2024 the famous souvenir sandals were retired (eco reasons). Bring water shoes or quick-dry shoes you don't mind soaking. Old sneakers, Tevas, anything not leather.
